**First client.** A clinic with one doctor and two stock pools in one building: **General Pharmacy** (GEN) and **Doctor's Medicines** (DOC, the OPD). The doctor owns both and dispenses from DOC against his own prescription, and also prescribes from the general pharmacy. All stock is received at GEN and transferred to DOC. There are two counters, one per pool, each with its own till; an OPD bill is a consultation fee plus one lump-sum charge for the medicines. The clinic charges no VAT on sales. It runs its own install and database, on a cloud server since the decision of 2026-10-22 (it was a local server in the first plan; [decisions](#client-answers-2026-10-20), [docs/deploy/CLOUD.md](deploy/CLOUD.md)).

**Phase 1** (this plan): drug master with schedules (OTC / prescription-only / controlled), pack and unit selling, batch and expiry stock with FEFO, two locations through `multi_location`, real suppliers and purchasing, pharmacy checkout and receipt, stock control, daily and monthly reports, Excel import.

**Later, not built now:** Phase 2 patients and prescriptions, Phase 3 bookings. This plan only leaves room for them to attach (see [Hooks for Phase 2 and 3](#hooks-for-phase-2-and-3)).

**Decided on the answers (2026-10-20, product owner):**

- **Cloud, not a local server (2026-10-22):** the clinic's install runs on a **cloud VPS** (Ubuntu 26.04 LTS, Singapore or Mumbai) behind the clinic's domain with HTTPS, with a production and a staging environment; the two till PCs (T1 GEN, T2 DOC/OPD) use it through Chrome and keep billing with the offline till during internet outages (broadband with 4G failover recommended). One app and database per client; several clients may share a VPS; the retail client the same way. This replaces the local server of the first plan everywhere below; branch 12 is now `chore/cloud-deployment` ([as built](#as-built-branch-12), [docs/deploy/CLOUD.md](deploy/CLOUD.md)).
- **Tills and accounts for the cloud install (2026-10-22):** the till never signs out by itself (idle sign-out off at the till, 0; the admin panel's 30 minutes), so an outage never stops billing (a till PIN lock that works offline is a future item); passwords of at least 12 characters for anyone with the admin panel and 10 for till-only users, never one known from a breach, no case or symbol rules; the clinic sells only with an open shift (the pharmacy profile's `require_open_shift`); no Discount button for a cashier whose limit is 0; the till's Stock requests link only at DOC's till (T2), not GEN's; staging recommended on its own small VPS ([as built](#as-built-branch-12)).

- **Discounts:** the pharmacy profile starts shops with `max_cashier_discount_percent` = 0 (built). The owner can still discount.
- **Q8, offline edge cases:** accept and flag, never reject: a sale by a disabled cashier (already built: review reason `cashier`) and a sale whose till clock is far in the future or the past. Both the device's and the server's time are kept, and reports use the server's time when the till's clock was out by more than a configurable tolerance. Design in [§7.5](#75-till-clock-and-disabled-cashiers-decided-2026-10-20). **Built in branch 8**, replay and reports both (reports reading `reported_at` moved from branch 11 to 8).
- **Q6, consultation fee:** pre-filled from the Consultation product's price and **editable at the OPD till** ([§4.3](#43-service-products-consultation-fee)). **Built in branch 8.**
- **Profit cost (2026-10-21):** profit reports and the dashboard's gross profit and margin use **each sale line's own stored cost** (`sale_items.unit_cost`), not the ledger's cost, so there is no rounding per tablet; returns take back their original line's cost. Stock valuation stays on the ledger and each location's weighted average. **Built in branch 11** ([as built](#as-built-branch-11)).
- **Dashboard profit below zero (2026-10-22):** "Gross profit today LKR -7,002.50" against "Sales today LKR 600.00" on the demo clinic is **genuine**, not a bug: the one OPD bill charged a Medicines amount of 100.00 for two vials of Insulatard (3,800.00 each at cost) and 1 ml of Chlorphenamine (2.50), with a 500.00 consultation, so revenue 600.00 less the lines' cost 7,602.50. Profit reads only the sale lines (opening stock, counts, write-offs and transfers never count; a refund counts once). The tile now shows revenue − cost and the margin under the figure, turns red below zero, and names the OPD medicines charged below cost. **Decided the same day:** the till shows the medicines' **list value** (Σ list price × quantity of the priced medicines; never a cost) next to the Medicines amount and gives a **soft, non-blocking warning** when the amount is below a shop-set share of it (`opd_below_list_warning_percent`: 100% by default, 0 = off, the owner sets it under Pharmacy checkout); the sale records the list value and that it was charged below it (`sales.opd_medicines_list_value`, `opd_below_list`), online and offline (see [as built (branch 11b)](#as-built-branch-11b)).
- **OPD receipt:** a shop setting chooses between **listing the medicine names without prices** (default) and **totals only** (consultation and Medicines amounts). The setting (`opd_receipt_medicines`) and both prints are built in branch 8; the receipt's layout is **built in branch 9** (the setting moved to Receipt settings).

### Unit defaults

Standard pharmacy practice; the selling rules were **decided on 2026-10-18 (Q16)** and the pack sizes and syrup units **confirmed by the clinic on 2026-10-20** ([client answers](#client-answers-2026-10-20)). Built as defaults the form and import suggest, so changing them later is a config change, not a migration (`config('pos.pharmacy.unit_defaults')`, built in branch 3).

| Dosage form              | Base unit        | Sold as                                     | Loose sale of the base unit (`allow_unit_sale`)                               |
| ------------------------ | ---------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Tablet, capsule          | Tablet / Capsule | loose, or by the box (30 or 100; 16 seen)   | on                                                                            |
| Syrup                    | ml               | the bottle; loose ml at Doctor's Medicines  | **off** by default; per location (stored since branch 7, sold since branch 8) |
| Suspension, drops        | Bottle           | the bottle (and a carton, if one is set up) | **off** by default (only matters once a carton is set up)                     |
| Cream, ointment, inhaler | Tube / Piece     | whole pack only                             | off                                                                           |
| Injection                | Vial / Ampoule   | single vial / ampoule, and the box          | on                                                                            |

- **Several pack levels per product** (tablet → strip → box), each with its own conversion factor to the base unit and **its own selling price**. The loose unit price is set independently; no price is ever derived from another unit's price.
- **The only unit is always sellable (Q16).** A product whose only unit is its base unit (a bottle of syrup with no carton, a tube) can always be sold; `allow_unit_sale` has no effect on it. `allow_unit_sale` only governs products with a larger pack (e.g. a carton of bottles), where it decides whether the single base unit is offered too.
- **No half tablets, no half packs** (changed 2026-10-18, replacing "whole base units"): every pack factor is a whole number, and every quantity sold, received, counted or returned is a whole number **in the unit it is entered in**: 1 strip or 3 tablets, never 0.5 box (even though half a box of 30 is 15 whole tablets).
- **Defaults are suggested by dosage form** in the medicine form and in the import, and are editable per product. The suggested packs are **Box of 30 and Box of 100** for tablets and capsules (the clinic's common sizes; a product normally keeps the one it comes in, and the import sets the real size), **Bottle of 100 ml** for syrup, Box of 10 for injections, none for the rest; gel is treated like cream, and `other` is a loose Piece. Two packs of one item need different names, hence "Box of 30" rather than "Box"; pickers show a pack named by its size as it is ("Box of 30", not "Box of 30 of 30", `VariantUnit::describe()`).
- **Syrup in ml (2026-10-20).** The clinic dispenses loose ml of syrup at Doctor's Medicines, so syrup is counted in ml and the bottle is a pack. General Pharmacy sells bottles only, so loose sale is off by default; per location (on at DOC) it is stored since branch 7 (import, product form: `variant_unit_locations`) and honoured at the till since branch 8 (online refused, offline flagged `unit`). A syrup with loose sale off and no rule for the location sells by the bottle only. Suspensions and drops stay counted in bottles until the clinic says otherwise.
- **A product's base unit cannot be changed once it has any stock movement.** Pack levels can still be added, re-priced, deactivated or have their factor changed (sale and purchase lines snapshot the factor).

## 3. Decisions in one place

1. **A medicine is a product with one variant.** Packs are **units** of that variant, not variants (§4.2).
2. **Stock is always held in the base unit** (tablet, capsule, ml or bottle). Packs convert by an exact factor.
3. **The two pools are two `locations`.** The pool is chosen **per sale line** (`sale_items.location_id`), not per sale (§4.4).
4. **Batches belong to the variant; their quantities are per location.** `stock_levels` stays the per-location total and the lock that serialises everything for that item at that location (§6).
5. **Costing: weighted average stays, but moves to per variant per location**; batches carry their own purchase cost for write-off valuation and supplier credits. All outflows leave at the location's average; transfers arrive at the source location's average (§5).
6. **Batch-tracking is a product property** (`products.track_batches`), switched on for every medicine. Non-batch items (consultation, sundries) run exactly as today.
7. **New flag `pharmacy`** for the drug master, schedules, batches UI and pharmacy checkout. Units become core (not under `wholesale`): a variant with only its base unit behaves exactly as today.
8. **Offline: the till proposes a batch allocation, the server decides on replay and records any difference; a paid sale is never rejected for a pharmacy rule** (§7).
9. **Install profile** (`retail` | `pharmacy`) chooses install-time defaults only: seeders, locations, roles and which modules `pos:install` switches on. Runtime on/off state lives in Pennant (§8).
10. **Tax category per product** (`exempt` | `standard`), medicines default `exempt` (§4.1).

## 4. Data model

Conventions as today: tenant tables have `shop_id` + `BelongsToShop`; records created on tills or exposed in URLs get `uuid` (`HasPublicUuid`); money is BIGINT minor units; quantities DECIMAL(14,3); FKs `restrictOnDelete` unless noted; ledger-like rows are never updated except counters such as `returned_quantity`.

**One deliberate exception to rule 2 (approved, Q9):** _costs per base unit_ are stored as DECIMAL(20,4) minor units. A box of 30 at LKR 1,000.00 is 3,333.3333 minor per tablet; integer minor units would lose up to 0.5 minor per tablet, which is 1% or more on cheap tablets. Selling prices, line totals, payments and every reported money total stay integers.

### 4.2 Products or variants? Units and packs

**Recommendation: one product per presentation (trade name + strength + form), with a single variant (`name = null`).** Strengths are not variant axes:

- Each strength has its own schedule, registration number, barcode, batches, supplier code and price; pharmacists search "amoxicillin 500".
- Variants each carry their own stock. Modelling packs as variants would split one shelf of tablets into separate stock counts; packs must share stock.
- Keeping one variant means the ledger, prices, barcodes, search, sync and reports need no new concept for medicines: they already work per variant.

Grouping (all paracetamol products) comes from `product_generics`, not from variants.

**`products`** addition: `whole_units_only` boolean default false; true for every medicine (set with `drug_details`). When on, `CheckoutService`, `StockService`, GRNs, transfers, stock takes and returns refuse any quantity that is not a whole number of the unit it is entered in (no half tablets, no half boxes). Retail keeps decimal quantities.

**Which units can be sold (`allow_unit_sale`):** the base unit row's `is_sellable`. It only matters when a larger pack exists; when the base unit is the product's only unit (a single bottle, a tube) it is always sellable (Q16, decided). Syrups (base unit ml, Bottle pack) default to `allow_unit_sale` off, so they sell by the bottle; injections default to on, so single vials and ampoules sell. **Per location (client answers, 2026-10-20):** the clinic wants loose syrup at Doctor's Medicines but not at General Pharmacy, so selling the base unit loose is a per-location choice. **Stored since branch 7** as a per-location override table, `variant_unit_locations` (`variant_unit_id`, `location_id`, `is_sellable`; unique pair; `shop_id`): a row overrides the unit's `is_sellable` at that location, no row means the product-wide flag. The medicine form fills them in when a dosage form is chosen on a new product, and the import applies them to rows that leave the unit columns blank. Every value is editable per product; nothing at runtime reads the config except these suggestions.

**Base unit is locked after the first movement.** `ProductCatalogService` refuses to change which unit is the base, or its name, once the variant has any `stock_movements` row (every quantity in the ledger, batches and sale lines is in that unit). Packs stay editable: adding, deactivating, re-pricing or changing a pack's factor affects only future lines, because sale, PO and GRN lines snapshot `unit_factor`. A wrong base unit after go-live is fixed by creating a new product and moving stock across with an adjustment pair.

#### As built (branch 2)

Built in `feat/pharmacy-units` as above, with these details settled in the code:

- **Base unit per product.** The base row's name is `products.unit` on every variant; renaming it renames every base row, and the lock checks movements on any of the product's variants. Packs are per variant (a medicine has one).
- **"The variant's price" means the base unit's price.** `ProductVariant::prices()` is limited to base-unit rows, so search, catalog sync, the products table and stock valuation read exactly what they read before. Pack prices are on `VariantUnit::prices()`. A pack saved without a price has no price row (it can't be sold), rather than a price of 0.
- **Q16 in code** (`ProductVariant::canSellUnit()`): a pack sells when active and `is_sellable`; the base unit sells when `allow_unit_sale` is on, **or when no active pack is sellable**. Deactivating the only carton makes the bottle sellable again.
- **Whole units of the unit entered** (`StockService::assertWholeUnits()`): with `whole_units_only` on, a quantity must be a whole number in the unit it is sold, received, counted or returned in. 1 strip and 3 tablets are fine; 0.5 box is refused although it is 15 whole tablets, and so is 1.5 strips.
    - **Checkout and quote:** the line's quantity in its sold unit.
    - **Stock In and stock counts:** entered in base units, so whole tablets. `StockService::record()` and `adjustTo()` also refuse part base units (the count must be whole; the correcting difference may not be).

### As built (branch 6)

Built in `feat/pharmacy-purchasing`, generic so a retail shop can switch it on (`pos:module enable purchasing`, which brings `suppliers`): batch and expiry fields appear and are required only for items with `track_batches`. Retail with the modules off sees no change.

- **Modules and permissions.** `suppliers` guards the Suppliers screen; `purchasing` guards orders, goods received, supplier returns, payments, balances and the print routes, and now **needs `suppliers`** (`pos.module_requires`). Two permissions instead of the draft's four: **`purchasing.manage`** (suppliers, orders, supplier returns, payments, reversing a GRN) and **`purchasing.receive`** (enter and post GRNs; separate for a future pharmacist role). Both are owner's only for now. Anyone with either can read suppliers, orders and GRNs; supplier returns, payments and balances need `purchasing.manage`.
- **Suppliers** (`suppliers`): name (unique per shop, any case), contact person, phone, email, address, tax / registration number, payment terms (0 to 365 days), notes, active. Deactivated, never deleted; audited. No `code` column and no `supplier_variants` table yet (the cost of a new line is suggested from the item's last purchase cost × the unit's factor).
- **Purchase orders** (`purchase_orders`, `purchase_order_items`, `PO-000001` per shop): supplier, deliver-to location, expected date, notes, lines in any unit the item is bought in (`is_purchasable`) with the expected cost per unit. Status `draft` → `sent` → `partially_received` → `received`, or `cancelled` (from draft, sent or partly received; what arrived stays received). Only drafts are edited; orders are never deleted, so numbers have no gaps. Each line counts what has arrived in base units (`received_base_quantity`): **paid quantity only, free goods come on top**. "Receive goods" on a sent order opens a GRN with its supplier, location and what is still to come (in the unit ordered, or in base units when a part pack is left).
- **Goods received** (`goods_receipts`, `goods_receipt_items`), with or without an order: supplier, location, received on, supplier invoice number and date, optional invoice discount, notes; lines in packs or base units with paid and free quantity, cost per unit, line discount, and for batch-tracked items the batch number, expiry (`03/2027`, `3/27`, `2027-03` or a full date, as everywhere) and "Accept short expiry". Saved as a **draft** (no stock moves, no number; editable, deletable), then **posted** (`GoodsReceiptService::post()`, one transaction): numbered `GRN-000001`; each line comes in through `StockService` as a `stock_in` referencing the GRN, in base units, into its batch (`receive()`: BatchService rules, short expiry needs the tick and `batches.manage`, one expiry per batch number, settles the location's unallocated stock first) or for items without batches through `record()`; the paid quantity is counted against the order. The same supplier invoice can't be posted twice (unless the first GRN was reversed). Posted GRNs never change (model guards on the GRN and its lines).
- **Effective cost, free goods, discounts and tax.** Per line: `gross = quantity × unit cost`; the invoice discount is spread over the lines by value after line discount (`Money::allocate`, the odd minor unit to the largest line); `line total = gross − line discount − discount share + line tax + tax share`; `base quantity = (paid + free) × unit factor`; **`cost per base unit = line total ÷ base quantity`**, exact to 4 places (Q9), stored on the line (`base_unit_cost`) and on a new batch, and what re-averages the location (§5). Free goods lower the cost of every unit: 10 boxes of 10 at 90.00 with 2 boxes free and 45.00 off is 855.00 over 120 tablets = 7.125 a tablet; with 30 tablets already at 10.00 the average becomes (30 × 10 + 120 × 7.125) ÷ 150 = 7.70.
- **Tax (VAT) on supplier invoices** (added on 2026-10-20: the clinic charges no VAT on sales, so the VAT it pays is part of the cost). A GRN takes tax **per line** (`goods_receipt_items.tax`, as the invoice prints it) **or once for the invoice** (`goods_receipts.invoice_tax`), never both (the same VAT would count twice: refused with a message). The invoice tax is spread over the lines like the invoice discount, by each line's value after both discounts (`invoice_tax_share`, the odd minor unit to the largest line; refused when no line has a cost to carry it). Tax goes into the line total, so into the cost per base unit, the batch's cost, the location's average and the supplier's balance: 855.00 of goods with 153.90 VAT (18%) over 120 tablets is 1,008.90 ÷ 120 = 8.4075 a tablet. The header keeps `line_tax_total` and `invoice_tax`; a reversing GRN negates them. Shown on the form (line tax, invoice tax, live totals), the GRN page and the printed GRN (a Tax column and the tax lines in the totals, only when there is tax). No tax is reclaimed or reported as input tax: there is no VAT return to feed.
- **Reversing GRN** (`reverse()`, `purchasing.manage`, a reason required): a new posted GRN (`GRN-000002`, `reversal_of_id`) whose lines are the original's negated; each line leaves **the batch it went into** as a `receipt_reversal` movement at its own cost (`StockService::reverseReceipt()`), and the location's average is **un-averaged exactly**: `(on hand × average − quantity × cost) ÷ (on hand − quantity)` (`UnitCost::withoutReceipt()`, never below zero; with nothing left the average stands), so reversing an untouched receipt restores the average it replaced. It is refused when the batch (or, without batches, the location) no longer holds all of it: sold or moved stock is a supplier return or a count. The order's received quantities go back down. The reversal counts in the month it was made. `pos:stock-rebuild --costs` replays reversals.
- **Supplier returns** (`supplier_returns`, `supplier_return_items`, `SR-000001`), posted when saved (`SupplierReturnService`): supplier, location, reason (expired, near expiry, damaged, recalled, other), date, notes, lines of item, **the batch** (any batch with stock at the location, expired, quarantined or recalled ones included), unit and quantity. Each line writes a `supplier_return` movement out of that batch (`StockService::returnToSupplier()`), **with the batch's purchase cost as the ledger cost**, and credits `base quantity × batch cost`; the location's average does not change (§5). Items without batches leave at the location's average. Only the supplier's credit note number can be added afterwards.
- **Supplier balance** (Q7, simple): opening balance + posted GRN totals (reversals negative) − supplier return credits − payments recorded (`supplier_payments`: date, amount, method, reference, notes). Shown on the supplier list and page with the supplier's opening balance, GRNs, returns and payments. No accounting integration. Per-GRN paid status, due dates and allocations came in branch 6b ([as built](#as-built-branch-6b)); the balance is worked out the same way.
- **Opening balance for go-live** (`supplier_opening_balances`, added after the first review): what a supplier was owed when the shop started using the system, entered once per supplier from its page (date, amount owed, note; a negative amount is a credit the supplier owed the shop; not zero, not in the future), `purchasing.manage`, audited, part of the balance. Never edited or deleted (model guards): a mistake is put right by **Reverse opening balance**, which writes an entry with the amount negated, dated today, with the reason (`reversal_of_id`); then the right one is entered. A second one is refused while one stands (the supplier row is locked, so two entered at once make one: MySQL group).
- **Payments are corrected the same way**: never edited or deleted; **Reverse** on a payment records a payment with the amount negated, dated today, with the reason (`supplier_payments.reversal_of_id`). The service always reverses the entry as stored, under the supplier's lock, once.
- **Items created while receiving** (added 2026-10-20, `ReceivingItemService`, `QuickItemForm`). The item field on order and GRN lines finds an item by name, generic, brand, strength ("amox 250", "500mg"), SKU or the barcode of any of its units; every word typed must match. A scanned barcode finds just its item, and a **pack's barcode also picks that pack** on the line. When nothing matches, **Create new item** opens a form started with what was typed (or the scanned barcode). A pharmacy gets the medicine essentials: medicine or not, name, generic names, brand name, manufacturer, strength, dosage form (which fills in the base unit, loose sale and packs, as in the product form), schedule, tax, barcode, packs, and optional prices (blank = no price row, so **not for sale yet**); a retail shop gets name, category, unit, barcode and cost. The new item is selected on its line and nothing else on the document changes.
    - **Duplicate guard.** A barcode already on another item or pack is refused outright. An item with the same generic names (any order or case), strength and dosage form, or a name that differs only in case, spacing, punctuation or a letter or two, is a warning answered on the form: **Use existing** (that item goes on the line, nothing is created) or **Create anyway**.
    - **Who.** `products.manage` creates a normal item (prices only with `prices.manage`). Someone with `purchasing.receive` or `purchasing.manage` but not `products.manage` creates it **`needs_review`**, without prices: it can be ordered and received, but the till's search, the offline catalog (a till that has it drops it) and online checkout leave it out until someone with `products.manage` uses **Mark as reviewed** on the product page (after setting its prices; reviewed but unpriced is still not for sale). An offline sale is never refused for it. Products has an **Items to review** filter and badge, and the dashboard an **Items to review** tile for product managers.
    - **Audit.** Besides the item's own `created` entry, a `created_while_receiving` entry names the document (`goods received note draft`, `purchase order PO-000003`, with its uuid) and whether it needs review. It is written as soon as the item is linked to its document (`products.created_on`): at once on a document being edited, or when a new document is first saved.
- **Where goods are received.** New orders and GRNs start at the user's work location, or, with the shop setting `receive_at_default_location` (Shop settings → Purchasing, shown with several locations; on in the pharmacy profile), at the shop's default location: the clinic receives everything at GEN and transfers to DOC. Supplier returns still start at the user's location.
- **Printing.** Purchase order, goods received note (posted only; a reversal says what it reverses) and supplier return note as A4 pages for the browser's print dialog (`/purchasing/orders/{uuid}/print` and so on), behind `auth`, the `purchasing` module and the document's policy; another shop's document is not found.
- **Reports hooks.** Purchases by supplier and month need nothing more: `goods_receipts` keeps the supplier, `received_on` (the month), the status and the totals (reversals negative), and its lines the item, quantities, base quantity and cost (index `(supplier_id, received_on)`); supplier returns keep the supplier, `returned_on` and the credit at batch cost, so purchase price variance against the location average can be reported. The stock movements report names the GRN or return of each movement; its type filters show the two new movement types only when the shop has purchasing.

Taking the shop row before any read matters on MySQL: the transaction's snapshot then includes the previous posting (the MySQL group shows a draft posted from several places at once is posted once, simultaneous deliveries against one order all count with distinct numbers, and a reversal racing the tills never takes a batch below zero).

### As built (branch 6b)

Built in `feat/pharmacy-payables` (client answers of 2026-10-20: credit terms are used; unpaid invoices and due dates must be tracked). Part of the `purchasing` module, generic for any shop; retail with purchasing off sees no change. All money is integer minor units.

- **Open items** (`supplier_payables`, `SupplierPayable`). Posting a GRN makes it an invoice owed to the supplier (a reversing GRN does not; a GRN worth nothing owes nothing), and so does each opening entry with a positive amount. Each keeps the supplier's invoice number and date, its document date, **due date**, amount, what has been allocated to it and its **status**: `unpaid`, `part_paid`, `paid`, or `reversed` (its GRN or opening entry was reversed: nothing is owed). Outstanding = amount − allocated (0 when reversed).

- **Paying.** **Record payment** (supplier page, or the Payables screen with a supplier picker; `purchasing.manage`): amount, date, method, reference, notes, and how it is set against invoices: **oldest due first** (the default, worked out when it is saved, under the lock), **choose invoices and amounts** (pre-filled oldest due first, editable, part payments allowed, one payment over several invoices), or **keep it all as credit**. ## 5. Costing

**Decision: keep weighted average cost, held per variant per location (`stock_levels.average_cost`). Batches carry their own purchase cost (`stock_batches.unit_cost`) for write-off valuation and supplier credits. Every outflow leaves at the location's average.**

Why per location:

- The same medicine can sit in both pools (one barcode, so one variant: `product_variants.barcode` is unique per shop). The two pools are stocked separately, and profit is reported per location so the doctor's share is visible (Q1). A shop-wide average would mix the doctor's purchase prices into the pharmacy's profit and the other way round. With one owner today this is about correct reporting; if the doctor is later settled separately, the figures are already right.
- For a one-location retail shop the per-location average equals today's average, so nothing changes for the moto-gear client.

Why not cost sales at batch cost (FIFO / specific identification):

- It would make retail and pharmacy cost differently, split every report into two code paths, and break the existing WAC rules and tests.
- Batch costs on the same item barely differ between deliveries; the information that matters (what an expired batch cost us, what credit to claim) is still kept on the batch.

How it works:

| Event                                  | Average at the location                                                         | Ledger `unit_cost`                                  |
| -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Opening, Stock In, GRN posting         | re-averaged with the receipt's base unit cost (existing formula, at 4 dp)       | receipt cost                                        |
| Transfer out                           | unchanged at the source                                                         | source average                                      |
| Transfer in                            | re-averaged at the destination with the **source location's** average (decided) | source average                                      |
| Sale, adjustment out, expiry write-off | unchanged                                                                       | location average                                    |
| Supplier return (branch 6)             | unchanged                                                                       | the batch's purchase cost                           |
| Reversing GRN (branch 6)               | un-averaged exactly with the receipt's own cost                                 | receipt cost                                        |
| Return, void                           | unchanged                                                                       | the original line's `unit_cost` ÷ factor (as today) |
| Revalue (`cost.manage`)                | set by hand, **per location**, audited                                          | —                                                   |

- Expiry write-off: the books lose `quantity × location average` (the movement), and the expiry report also shows `quantity × batch unit_cost` ("what this batch cost us") so the owner can claim from the supplier.
- Supplier return: the credit expected is at the batch cost, and (as built in branch 6) so is the ledger movement; the difference from the location average is the purchase price variance the supplier return report can show. The average is **not** re-averaged on the way out (avoids nonsense averages when little stock is left).
- `product_variants.cost_price` stays, maintained by `StockService` as the blended average across locations (Σ qty × avg ÷ Σ qty), so the product form, audit history and retail screens keep working. `last_cost_price` keeps its meaning.
- Lock order is unchanged in spirit: a receipt locks the variant row (only to update the blended figure), then the stock level; sales lock only stock levels (§6.3).

Effect on existing reports:

| Report                                                      | Change                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| ----------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Profit by product, gross profit, dashboard profit           | Figures unchanged: they use `sale_items.unit_cost`, still snapshotted at sale time (now from the line's location). They gain a **per-location breakdown** (`sale_items.location_id`), required for the doctor's share (Q1).          |
| Stock valuation, stock value at cost, dashboard stock value | Switch from `Σ on_hand × variant.cost_price` to `Σ max(level, 0) × level.average_cost`, with a location filter/column. Same numbers for single-location shops.                                                                       |
| Stock movements report                                      | None (movement `unit_cost` keeps its meaning); gains location and batch columns.                                                                                                                                                     |
| Low stock                                                   | Uses `COALESCE(stock_levels.reorder_level, variant.reorder_level)` per location.                                                                                                                                                     |
| Sales by day / cashier / category, payment methods          | Gain a location filter and per-location columns via `sale_items.location_id`. Sale-level figures (payments, change) stay per bill; a bill-level discount is already spread over lines, so per-location net sales add up to the bill. |

- **Transfers** (Stock → Transfers, `stock.transfer`, owner; readable with `stock.view`): `stock_transfers` (`TR-000001` per shop, from, to, note, user, time) and `stock_transfer_items` (unit and factor snapshot, quantity in the unit entered, base quantity, source average, both movement ids). `StockTransferService::create()` posts on save, in one transaction: any active unit of the item (box, strip, loose), whole units of it for a whole-units product, from and to active and different (a non-sellable location such as quarantine is allowed), never more than the source has. ## 6. FEFO allocation inside StockService

### 6.1 API

`record()` keeps its signature for non-batch items. Batch-tracked items go through two new methods that share the same private movement writer:

```php
// Outflows (sale, transfer out, write-off, supplier return): picks batches.
StockService::issue(
    ProductVariant $variant, Location $location, StockMovementType $type, float $baseQuantity,
    ?User $user, ?Model $reference, ?BatchAllocation $proposal = null,   // offline till's plan, or a batch the user picked
    bool $allowNegative = false, ?\DateTimeInterface $occurredAt = null, ...
): BatchAllocation   // list of [batch, quantity, movement] + differences from the proposal

// Inflows (opening, GRN, return, void, transfer in): into a named batch.
StockService::receive(ProductVariant $variant, Location $location, StockBatch $batch, StockMovementType $type, float $baseQuantity, ?int|string $unitCost, ...): StockMovement
```

`record()` refuses a batch-tracked variant (so nothing can bypass batches), the same way it refuses a variant of another shop today.

### 6.2 Allocation (one sale line)

Inside one transaction:

1. **Lock the stock level** `(location, variant)`: the existing `lockLevel()`. This row is the mutex for the item at this location, so every batch read after it is consistent and two tills can never take the same units.
2. **Load sellable batch levels** for `(location, variant)`, joined to `stock_batches`, `FOR UPDATE`:
    - `quantity > 0`, `status = active`, not `is_unallocated`;
    - `expiry_date IS NULL OR expiry_date >= sale date + min_days_to_expiry` (drug override, else shop setting `min_days_to_expiry`, default 0);
    - order by `expiry_date IS NULL`, `expiry_date`, `stock_batches.id` (soonest first, non-expiring last, oldest batch breaks ties).
3. **Apply the proposal first, if any** (a batch the pharmacist picked, or the offline till's plan): each proposed `(batch, qty)` is honoured when the batch is in the list above and has the quantity; whatever is invalid or short is dropped from the plan and recorded as a difference.
4. **Fill the rest FEFO**: walk the list, take `min(available, remaining)` from each batch until the line is covered. **This is the multi-batch split**: 25 tablets with 10 left in batch A (exp. 2026-11) and 200 in B (exp. 2027-03) gives A 10 + B 15.
5. **Shortfall**:
    - online sale, `allow_negative_stock` off: throw `insufficientStock`, saying how much is held back as expired / quarantined so the cashier knows why;
    - offline replay (or negative stock allowed): put the remainder on the variant's **unallocated** batch at that location (§6.4) and add a `batch_shortfall` review reason.
6. **Write one movement per piece**, each with `stock_batch_id`, signed quantity, `balance_after` running across the pieces (level − A, then level − A − B), `batch_balance_after`, `unit_cost` = location average. Update each `batch_levels` row and the `stock_levels` row once.
7. Return the allocation; `CheckoutService` writes one `sale_item_batches` row per piece with its movement id.

`CheckoutService` already sorts lines by variant id before touching stock; it now sorts by `(location_id, product_variant_id)`. Two lines of the same variant at the same location (box + loose tablets) are allocated one after the other under the same lock, so the second sees what the first took.

### 6.3 Lock order (no new deadlocks)

`product_variants` row (receipts only) → `stock_levels` row(s) in `(location_id, variant_id)` order → that level's `batch_levels` rows in FEFO order. Batch rows are only ever locked while their level row is held, so two transactions can never lock batches in conflicting orders. Transfers lock source and destination levels in `location_id` order, read the source average while holding the source lock, issue FEFO from the source and receive the same batches at the destination at that cost.

### 6.4 The unallocated batch (oversold stock)

Offline tills, and shops that allow negative stock, can sell more than the system holds. The units existed physically (most often a delivery that is on the shelf but not yet entered). Each variant gets one sentinel batch (`is_unallocated = true`, created on first need) whose level may go below zero.

When stock is next received for that variant at that location, `receive()` settles the sentinel first: a `batch_correction_out` from the new batch and `batch_correction_in` to the sentinel for `min(received, −sentinel)`, net zero on the level. The oversold units are then attributed to the batch that arrived. A stock take can also settle it.

**Alert (decided):** whenever an unallocated batch level goes below zero, the sale gets a `batch_shortfall` review reason and the owner and pharmacists see an alert (dashboard widget "Unallocated stock" with item, location and quantity, plus a Filament database notification). The alert clears when the level is back to zero. The unallocated batch is **excluded from all expiry reports** and never offered to FEFO.

### 6.5 Returns and voids go back to the original batch

- **Void:** every `sale_item_batches` row is reversed exactly: a `sale_void` movement into the same batch at the same location, at the line's unit cost.
- **Return:** the returned base quantity is spread over the line's allocations that still have `quantity − returned_quantity > 0`. If the line used one batch (the common case) there is nothing to choose. If it was split, the return form shows the batches (they are on the receipt) and the user picks; the default is the latest-expiring allocation first. Each piece writes a `return` movement into that batch at `sale_items.location_id` (today: `sale.location`, which is wrong once lines have their own pool), and updates `sale_item_batches.returned_quantity` and `sale_items.returned_quantity`.
- A batch that has expired or been recalled since the sale still takes the units back (the ledger must balance) but they are not sellable (status / expiry); the stock screen shows them for write-off or supplier return.
- **Restock default** for medicines: off for `drug_details.storage_condition = fridge` (stored since branch 3) and for controlled drugs (Q5); the return form pre-ticks "restock" from it and the user can still change it. A medicine with no storage condition set restocks by default, as today. Not restocked = refund only, no movement, as today. Built with returns to batch in branch 5. **Confirmed by the clinic (2026-10-20):** fridge items are decided per return, which is what the form's "Put back into stock?" choice does (off by default for fridge items). Since branch 9 the choice is **per line** of a return, each line defaulting from its own item.

### 7.5 Till clock and disabled cashiers (decided 2026-10-20)

Answers Q8: **accept and flag for review, never reject.** **Built in branch 8**, offline payload, replay and reports (reports reading `reported_at` moved here from branch 11).

- **Disabled cashier:** already built. A sale by a cashier who is disabled or no longer has `pos.checkout` is recorded with review reason `cashier`. A cashier who is not a user of the shop is still rejected (§7.2).
- **Both clocks are kept.** `sales.sold_at` stays what the till's clock said (what the receipt printed); `sales.synced_at` already records when the server received an offline sale. Each upload also carries the till's clock at sending (`device_time`), and the server stores the difference from its own clock on each sale it replays (`sales.clock_offset_seconds`; null for online sales and for uploads from an older client).
- **Out of tolerance: flag, not reject.** A shop setting `till_clock_tolerance_minutes` (default 10, today's hard-coded skew) replaces the rejection of "sale time in the future". When the offset (or, without one, how far `sold_at` is ahead of the server) is beyond it, in either direction, the sale is recorded and flagged with review reason `clock`, saying by how much.
- **Reports use server time when the clock was out.** A new indexed `sales.reported_at`: `sold_at` when the clock was within tolerance (every online sale), otherwise the sale's time on the server's clock, which is `sold_at` corrected by the measured offset, or `synced_at` when no offset was measured. `ReportService`, the dashboard and day boundaries read `reported_at` instead of `sold_at`. A backfill sets it to `sold_at` on every existing sale, so retail reports don't move. FEFO and expiry still use the till's sale day (what physically happened), as built in branch 5.
- **As built (branch 8).** The till sends `device_time` (its clock, ISO 8601 with an offset) with every upload to `POST /pos/sync/sales`; `OfflineSaleSyncService::sync($device, $sales, $deviceTime)` measures the offset once per upload (device time − server time, whole seconds, positive = the till is ahead) and gives it to every sale in it. The shop setting **`till_clock_tolerance_minutes`** (default 10; Shop settings → Offline tills, 1 to 1,440) decides: beyond it in either direction, or, without `device_time` (an older client), a `sold_at` more than the tolerance **ahead** of the server, the sale is recorded and flagged `clock` ("The till's clock was 30 minutes ahead. Reports date the sale by the server's clock.", with `offset_seconds`). `sales.clock_offset_seconds` is stored for every offline sale with a measured offset, within tolerance too (null online and from older clients). **A sale can't have happened after it reached the server:** when `sold_at` − offset (or `sold_at`, without an offset) is still more than the tolerance after `synced_at`, the sale is flagged `clock` too, even with the offset within tolerance ("The sale time was 4320 minutes after the sale reached the server: the till's clock changed before it sent the sale."; a till clock fast while offline and put right by NTP just before the upload). **A time no TIMESTAMP column holds** (MySQL: 1970 to 2038-01-19; a clock that jumped to 2040 or was reset to 1970) is never inserted, which used to fail the insert and leave the sale retrying for ever: `sold_at` and `reported_at` are the arrival time, flagged `clock` ("The till recorded the sale at 2040-03-01 10:00, a time that can't be stored."); the offset is capped at what `clock_offset_seconds` (INT) holds. `sales.reported_at` = `sold_at` when not flagged (`Sale` fills it on create), `sold_at` − offset when flagged with an offset (unless that lands after the sale arrived or outside the column's range), `synced_at` otherwise. The rejection "The sale time is in the future" (and the fixed 10-minute skew) is removed. **Reports moved to `reported_at` in this branch, not branch 11:** `ReportService::sales()` (summary, by cashier, by category or brand, payment methods, best sellers, profit) and `salesByDay()`, and through them the dashboard widgets and report pages; the receipt, the recent-sales panel and the admin sales list keep `sold_at`, and voids, returns, shifts and stock movements keep their own times. The sale page shows the till's clock offset ("12 minutes ahead") and "Counted in reports at" when `reported_at` differs from `sold_at`.

## 8. Seeders and install: feature-aware

Today `DatabaseSeeder` always runs `VehicleSeeder` (global vehicle makes/models, no `shop_id`), and in `local`/`testing` the moto-gear demo shop. `migrate:fresh --seed` on `pos_pharmacy_dev` therefore gets vehicle data and a moto shop.

**Built in branch 1.** As below, with two differences from the first draft: the pharmacy profile also starts `offline_sync` (Q10), and it lists only the `owner` and `cashier` roles until the pharmacist and doctor permissions exist. Dosage forms are an enum (branch 3; routes are not built yet), so there is no `PharmacyReferenceSeeder` yet.

**Install profiles** in `config/pos.php`. A profile is a set of **install-time defaults only** (decided): it says what `db:seed` and `pos:install` create and which modules `pos:install` switches on for a new shop. After that, whether a feature is on is **only** Pennant's per-shop state (`$shop->hasModule()`, `module:` middleware, `pos:module enable|disable`). No runtime code reads `POS_PROFILE`; changing it on a running install changes nothing until the next seed or install.

        'business_type' => 'retail',

        'demo_seeder' => DemoShopSeeder::class,

    'pharmacy' => [
        'business_type' => 'pharmacy',
        'modules' => ['pharmacy', 'multi_location', 'suppliers', 'purchasing', 'offline_sync'], // offline_sync: Q10
        'reference_seeders' => [], // PharmacyReferenceSeeder when there is reference data to seed
        'demo_seeder' => DemoClinicSeeder::class,
        'locations' => [
            ['code' => 'GEN', 'name' => 'General Pharmacy', 'default' => true],

- `DatabaseSeeder`: roles and permissions always; then the active profile's `reference_seeders`; then its `demo_seeder` in `local`/`testing` only. Each reference and demo seeder also guards itself against the profile it is run under (`VehicleSeeder`, `DemoShopSeeder` and `DemoClinicSeeder` do nothing unless the profile lists them), so `db:seed --class=…` can't leak data either. This is a seed-time check (`InstallProfile::seeds()`), not a feature switch.
- **Demo clinic** (`DemoClinicSeeder`, shop code `CLINIC`): General Pharmacy (`GEN`, default) and Doctor's Medicines (`DOC`), and since branch 10 Quarantine / Expired (`QUAR`, does not sell), Retail price list, till T1 at GEN, `owner@clinic.test` and `cashier@clinic.test` (password `password`), the pharmacy profile's modules and settings on, no products and no vehicle data.
- **Profile settings** (added 2026-10-20): a profile may list `settings`, the shop settings a new shop starts with over `pos.shop_defaults` (`InstallProfile::settings()`, written by `ShopSetupService::provision()`, so by `pos:install` and the demo seeders; anything the shop was created with wins). The pharmacy profile starts shops with `near_expiry_warning_days` = 90, `receive_at_default_location` on, `max_cashier_discount_percent` = 0 (no discounts), and since branch 8 `rx_prescriber` = `required` and `bank_transfer_reference_required` on; retail has none, so its shops are unchanged. Like everything in a profile, they are starting values only: Shop settings changes them afterwards, and an existing shop is not touched (the dev clinic picks them up at the next `migrate:fresh --seed`).
- `pos:install --profile=pharmacy` (or a prompt, defaulting to `POS_PROFILE`): sets `shops.business_type`, creates the profile's locations, price list and till, and stores the new shop's module state in Pennant: the profile's modules (and what they need) on, every other module off. From then on modules are changed with `pos:module`, never by editing the profile. The demo seeders use the same `ShopSetupService::provision()`.
- **Module dependencies** (`config('pos.module_requires')`): `pharmacy` needs `suppliers`, `purchasing` and `multi_location`. `ModuleService` is the only place module state changes (`pos:module`, `pos:install`, seeders): enabling `pharmacy` enables the other three, and disabling any of them is refused while `pharmacy` is on. Disabling `pharmacy` leaves them on.
- **Vehicle UI behind a flag.** New module `vehicle_fitment` guards `/pos/vehicles`, the vehicle filter in item search and the offline catalog, the Vehicle makes resource and the fitment field. Its Pennant default resolver is `fn (Shop $shop) => $shop->business_type === 'retail'`, so the existing client keeps it on with no data migration (Pennant stores the resolved value on first check); after that the stored Pennant value is what counts. Off means: `/pos/vehicles` returns 404, item search ignores `make_id` / `model_id` / `year`, the offline catalog sends `fitments: []` and `vehicles: null`, the till hides its Vehicle button, the Vehicles admin resource is inaccessible, and the product form hides "Fits vehicles" (saving a product then leaves its fitments untouched). The part number and OEM fields stay.
- **Tests:** `phpunit.xml` pins `POS_PROFILE=retail` (forced in both `<env>` and `<server>`, so a shell or `.env` value can't leak in) so the existing suite is unchanged; pharmacy tests use a `PharmacyTestCase` that seeds the pharmacy profile.
- **Dev:** this worktree's `.env` needs `POS_PROFILE=pharmacy` before the first `migrate:fresh --seed` on `pos_pharmacy_dev`.
- **Roles per profile:** permissions stay one list (harmless where a module is off); only the roles a profile lists are created, so a retail shop never sees a Doctor role. A role that already exists is still re-synced with `config('pos.roles')` whatever the profile, so permission changes reach it.

New permissions (draft): `pharmacy.dispense_rx`, `pharmacy.dispense_controlled`, `pharmacy.controlled_register`, `batches.manage` (quarantine, recall, correct expiry), `stock.transfer`, `stock.write_off`, `pos.price_override` (if Q6; built in branch 8, with `pos.opd_bill`, both given to cashiers). Purchasing (built in branch 6) has two instead of the draft's four: `purchasing.manage` and `purchasing.receive` ([§4.9, as built](#as-built-branch-6)). Draft roles: **pharmacist** (checkout incl. Why these changes:

- **Profile and seeders first, units separate.** Every later branch adds seed data and pharmacy-only UI; the profile must exist before them or the dev DB and tests fill with vehicle data. ### As built (branch 8)

Built in `feat/pharmacy-checkout` (the plan's `feature/pharmacy-checkout`). The server enforces every rule online; a sale an offline till already made is recorded and flagged, never rejected for a pharmacy rule ([§7.2, as built](#72-review-reasons-never-rejections)). With `pharmacy` off the retail till behaves as before (below).

- **Tills and allowed locations** ([§4.4, as built](#44-locations)). `location_user`: with rows, only those listed (the owner too); with none, the shop's default location only, or every location for a user with `locations.all` (the owner) or in a shop without `multi_location` (retail); set on the user form, changed only by `LocationService::setAllowedLocations()` (audited). A till is fixed to its location: the picker lists only the user's tills with their location, every request that names a till refuses one elsewhere ("You can't use till T2: it is at Doctor's Medicines."), and a remembered till the user may no longer use is forgotten. Online checkout refuses a sale at a location the cashier may not use ("You can't sell at General Pharmacy."); offline replay records it with one `location` review reason. Each line is sold from the till's location, so the clinic's bills never mix pools.
- **Search payload** (`GET /pos/search` and catalog v2, `ItemSearchService::present()`). Existing keys keep their meaning (`price` = the base unit's price, `stock` = base units at the till's location). New:
    - `units`: the active units, base first, each with id, name, factor, barcode, its own price on the default list, `is_base`, `is_default_sale` and `sellable` (`canSellUnitAt()` at the till's location);
    - `sellable`: some unit sold here has a price (an item that isn't is greyed out at the till, "Not sold here" or "No price");
    - `whole_units_only`, `track_batches`, `allow_price_override` (true only for a product that allows it and does not track stock), `min_days_to_expiry` (the medicine's own; null = the shop's);
    - `batches` (batch-tracked items only): at the till's location, holding stock, active and not expired today, in first expiry first out order, with id, batch number, expiry and quantity in base units;
    - `nearest_expiry`: the first batch still sellable today with the item's minimum days;
    - `scanned_unit_id`: a scan also matches an active pack's barcode, and the item comes back with that pack (null for the variant's own barcode or SKU, and in free-text results).

    Everything is eager loaded, so a search or catalog page runs a fixed number of queries whatever it finds (a retail search runs two more than before; the batch query runs only when an item tracks batches). No costs.

- **Units at the till.** Cart lines are keyed by item and unit, so one item can be on a bill as a box and as loose tablets. The unit picker shows only when more than one unit sells here; choosing a unit the cart already has merges the lines. A scanned pack is added in that pack; otherwise the till adds the default-sale unit if it sells here, else the base unit, else the first pack that does. Online the unit must sell **at the line's location** ("SYR-1 is not sold by the ml at General Pharmacy."), in whole units of the unit sold for a whole-units product. Stock on the line is compared in base units. Search shows stock as packs and loose units ("2 × Box of 30 + 5 Tablet"), an item sold by default in a pack as "price / Box of 30", and medicines with their schedule badge, strength, form and generic names.
- **Batches on the line.** The till sends its `device` with every quote. With it, `CheckoutService::quote()` checks units at the till's location and returns, for each batch-tracked line, the batches first expiry first out would take today and any `shortfall`, from `BatchPlanner::plan()`: read only, no locks, the same batches and order as an online `StockService::issue()` (both now use `StockBatch::sellableOn()`, behaviour unchanged), a pick first, lines of the same item taking batches in turn. The unallocated batch is never on either list. The menu item shows how many batches have expired.

Posting is the supplier return of branch 6.

The **printable count sheet** is in shelf order with the packs of each item and, unless blind, what the system holds. **Counting** on screen: each line in base units, or **in a pack** (Count in packs; the unit and quantity are kept), whole units for whole-units items; **Add a line** for an item or a batch not on the sheet, a batch on record or one **found on the shelf** (number and expiry as printed, any real date). A blind count hides what the system holds on the counting screen too. **Snapshot (decided before merge):** when a line is counted, the count keeps what the system held for that line at that moment (`counted_system_quantity`: its batch, or the item without batches), and **counting the line again takes a new snapshot**; clearing it clears the snapshot. **Review** (owner) shows each counted line's snapshot (System when counted), the count, what the system holds now (System now) and the **variance = counted − snapshot**, with its **value at the location's average cost**, and the totals found, missing and net. **Posting** writes each counted line's variance as a **count correction** on what the system holds then (`StockService::adjustBy()` / `adjustBatchBy()`, referencing the count) in **one transaction**, items in id order (the order stock levels are locked in, so posting never deadlocks with tills), and records on each line what it held at posting, the variance written, the cost and value; uncounted lines are left as they are. **The rule while a count is open:** a count never blocks sales, transfers or receipts, and **what moves between counting a line and posting is not a variance**: 20 counted on a shelf of 20, then 3 sold, posts no variance and leaves 17; 8 counted on a batch of 10 that then receives 5 posts −2 and leaves 13. A variance never takes a batch below zero (if the batch sold out meanwhile it goes to zero), nor an item without batches further below zero. A batch-tracked item whose lines on the count were **all counted** also has its **unallocated stock** settled: what it was when the item's last line was counted (snapshot too) is counted back towards zero at posting (a line "Unallocated" is added); sold beyond the batches after that stays unallocated; an item with a line not counted keeps it. Cancelling a count changes no stock.

### As built (branch 7b)

Built in `feat/labels` (the plan's `feature/labels`), on the client's answer of 2026-10-20: most items have a manufacturer barcode; the rest need system-generated barcodes and printed labels. The `labels` module is generic: the clinic labels medicines and boxes without a manufacturer barcode; a spare-parts shop prints shelf and item labels. The **pharmacy profile switches it on** (`pos:install --profile=pharmacy`, the demo clinic); any other shop can with `php artisan pos:module enable labels --shop=CODE`. With it off nothing changes: no menu items, actions, filters or label routes (404), and no templates are created.

- **Barcodes** (`App\Support\Barcode`, `BarcodeService`). The format is **EAN-13 in the GS1 in-store range**, not Code 128, because EAN-13 scans on every scanner and at the till. An item (its base unit) is `200` + its variant id in 9 digits + the check digit, as the product form and the import already gave since branches 3 and 7. A pack is `201` + its unit id. Code 128 is only used to print a manufacturer's or typed code that is not a valid EAN-13, EAN-8 or UPC-A. The check digit is GS1 mod 10 (`gtinCheckDigit()`, for EAN-13, EAN-8 and UPC-A).
    - **Unique across items and packs** (deleted items too): a code already taken (a barcode typed in the 2xx range) moves to the next prefix, 202 to 299. The product form and the import still refuse a barcode already on another item or pack.
    - **Never overwritten:** only an item or pack with no barcode gets one. A pack gets its barcode when its labels first print (allowed with `labels.print`), or in bulk from the product list (**Give in-store barcodes**, `products.manage`). Either way the change is audited as a change to the unit, and the till's catalog picks it up through `updated_at`. The base unit always scans with the item's barcode.
    - `Barcode::isInStore()` (a valid EAN-13 starting with 2) tells an in-store code from a manufacturer's. The product list's filter **Without a manufacturer barcode** lists products with an active item or pack that has no barcode or an in-store one.
- **Bars** (`Labels\BarcodeSymbol`): encoded on the server by **picqer/php-barcode-generator** (v3, LGPL-3.0; no external service) and drawn as an SVG at an exact size in mm, every bar a whole number of modules.
- **Templates** (`label_templates`; Administration → **Label templates**; `labels.manage`, owner only). A template has a name and a default flag, and:
    - **Size** from presets: thermal rolls of 38 × 25, 50 × 25, 50 × 30, 40 × 30 and 58 × 40 mm; A4 sheets of 3 × 8 (70 × 37 mm), 4 × 10 (48.5 × 25.4 mm) and 5 × 13 (38.1 × 21.2 mm). Or a custom size: width and height and, for a sheet, labels across and down, page size, top and left margins, and gaps.
    - A blank edge inside each label, and the **printer's resolution** (203, 300 or 600 dpi).
    - **Fields:** shop name (optionally a shorter name of its own), item name, strength (pharmacy), pack ("Box of 30"), SKU, part number, barcode, the number under the bars, price, batch (pharmacy) and expiry (pharmacy).
    - The form has a **live preview** of two sample labels (a long medicine name with every value, or a spare part) at twice their size, with the bar width in dots and the smallest text.
    - A shop's first templates are created when labels are first used. The clinic gets **Medicine label** (the default: 38 × 25 roll with shop name, name, strength, pack, barcode and number, price and batch; **expiry off**, as the doctor asked for receipts on 2026-10-21) and **A4 sticker sheet** (5 × 13). Any other shop gets **Item label** (50 × 25 roll with name, pack, SKU, part number, barcode and number, and price; no shop name) and **A4 sticker sheet** (4 × 10).
    - One template is the default. The last template can't be deleted, and past prints keep the name of the template they used.
- **Readable and scannable** (`Labels\LabelLayout`, `config('pos.labels')`).
    - **Bars first.** The narrowest bar (the module) is a whole number of printer dots, as wide as the label allows up to 0.40 mm and **never under 0.25 mm**: 2 dots at 203 dpi, 3 at 300, 6 at 600, about 76% of EAN-13's nominal 0.33 mm. The bars sit inside the blank edge, with their quiet zones (11 and 7 modules for EAN-13) on the label, and are 5 to 15 mm high.
    - **Then the text**, in Arial. When it doesn't leave room for the bars, the largest text shrinks first, half a point at a time, down to **6 pt**; then the name goes from two lines to one, then is cut short with an ellipsis. Batch and expiry share the price's row, or get their own row. Widths are estimated from Arial's character widths, and the page also clips every line, so nothing spills off a label.
    - A template is **saved only when the samples fit**: sizes in range, the labels on the sheet, the text, and a scannable barcode. Otherwise the preview and the save say why, for example "Too much to print on a 50 × 12 mm label …" or "The barcode … is too long for a 25 mm wide label: it needs 28.3 mm to scan".
    - At print time, a label whose barcode can't fit (a long Code 128) prints the number without bars, and the print page says so.
- **Printing** (`LabelService::print()`, `labels.print`). A print is logged in `label_prints`: who, when, the template, where from, how many labels, and the items with their unit, batch, copies and barcode. It opens in a new tab as its **print preview** (`/labels/prints/{id}`, `LabelPrintController`).
    - Every page is exactly one label on a roll (`@page { size: 38mm 25mm; margin: 0 }`) or one sheet (A4, labels placed by the template's margins and gaps). So **any label printer's driver** prints it from the browser's print dialog, set to the label's paper size, margins None and scale 100%; the page says so.
    - On screen there is a zoom (rolls), a choice of another template, and **Start at label** for a part-used sheet.
    - Names and prices are read when the print opens, so a reprint from the log shows today's.
    - At most 1,000 copies of one label, and 2,000 labels in one print.
- **Where to print from.** Every source opens a modal: choose the template, then **Print** or **Add to print queue**.
    - **Product page:** each active unit and pack of each variant. One label of the unit a scan adds, none of the others until a quantity is typed, and an optional batch per line for a batch-tracked item.
    - **Posted goods received note** (**Print labels for this delivery**): each line in the pack it came in, as many as were received (paid + free, rounded up to whole labels), with the line's batch and expiry. A draft or a reversing GRN has none.
    - **Batches screen:** the selected batches, in the largest pack or the base unit, one label each or one per pack on hand. Never the unallocated batch.
    - **Product list:** any selection, labelling each item, each pack or both, with a number of copies of each.
- **Print queue** (Catalog → **Print labels**; `label_queue_items`, one queue per user): labels collected from any of the sources above. The same item, unit and batch is added up. Copies can be edited and lines removed. The queue prints all at once on one template (one log entry, then the queue is emptied), or can be emptied without printing. The menu shows how many labels are waiting.
- **Print log** (Catalog → **Label print log**): each user sees their own prints and the owner (`labels.manage`) sees everyone's. Any print can be opened and printed again.
- **Permissions** (`config/pos.php`, synced by a migration): **`labels.print`** (owner; can be given to a cashier, who then also needs `admin.access`, and `products.view` for the product list) and **`labels.manage`** (owner). Prints are not audited; the log is enough. Barcodes given are audited as catalog changes.
- **Scanning round trip:** every printed barcode is the item's or the pack's own. The till (`pos.search` online, and the offline catalog's barcodes) adds that item in that unit, and order and GRN search (`ReceivingItemService::search()`) picks the item and the pack. ### As built (branch 7)

- **Flow** (Administration → Imports, `ImportService`): **upload** (.xlsx or .csv with commas, semicolons or tabs; 20,000 rows and 20 MB at most) → **match the columns**: suggested from the shop's saved mapping, then the template's headings, then other names ("Qty", "Exp", "MRP", "Item code"); each field picks a column or none; required fields must be chosen; the mapping is saved per shop and type, and a file with the same headings gets it exactly (`import_mappings`) → **check** (the dry run: every row read and checked as the write would, nothing written): counts (create, update, skip, errors, warnings), the rows with problems, an **error report** (.xlsx: row number, the original cells under the file's own headings, Errors, Warnings; fixable and re-uploadable as it is), and a note when the same file (sha256) was imported before → **confirm** (only with no errors: a file goes in whole or not at all) → the `RunImport` job writes it.

Warnings: short expiry, a possible duplicate medicine (same generic names, strength and dosage form, on file or earlier in the file) or the same name as an item on file, a price left blank (that unit is not for sale until priced), an invoice already overdue, an invoice number a GRN already carries, what is skipped or updated.
- **All or nothing, re-runnable.** Confirming queues the write; it locks the shop row (imports of a shop run one at a time and see each other's results), **checks every row again** against what is on file now, and writes the whole file **in one transaction**: any row that now has an error, or that a service refuses ("Row 14: …"), fails the run and nothing is written; it can be checked and confirmed again. Rows already on file are matched (items by SKU, else barcode, else exact name when the row has neither; suppliers by name; opening stock by item, location and batch, or item and location without batches; opening invoices by supplier and number) and **skipped, or updated** for items and suppliers if the user chose so; updating takes only the filled-in cells (a medicine's blank cells keep its drug details) and never changes the base unit of an item with stock movements. Nothing is created twice, also when the same file is imported from two places at once (MySQL group).

Locally on MySQL 8.4, 5,000 medicines (each with a pack, a reorder level, a loose-sale rule, category and manufacturer) check in about 3 s and import in about 2.7 minutes; 10,000 opening stock rows check in about 4 s and import in about 2 minutes (`POS_IMPORT_BENCH=1 composer test:mysql`). The clinic's server needs a queue worker (`php artisan queue:work`; `composer run dev` already runs `queue:listen`), and with more than one worker `DB_QUEUE_RETRY_AFTER` above the longest import. The transaction holds the stock levels it writes until it ends, so large opening stock files go in before the tills open.
- **Import log** (`import_runs`, `ImportRun`): who, when, the file (kept under `storage/app/private/imports/{shop}`, downloadable), type, status (uploaded, checked, waiting, importing, imported, failed), rows, the check's and the result's counts, why it failed, and the error report. ## 11. Risks

| Risk                                                                                                                                                                   | Mitigation                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **SQLite tests don't test locking.** `lockForUpdate()` is a no-op on SQLite, so FEFO races and deadlock ordering are untested; SQLite DECIMAL is also not exact.       | A `mysql` test group for `StockService` concurrency, FEFO and costing at 4 dp. **Built in branch 5** (`composer test:mysql`, locally against `pos_pharmacy_test`, and in CI by the `mysql-locking` job on a MySQL 8.4 service); backfill-migration tests are still to do.                |
| Two cost figures (`stock_levels.average_cost` and the blended `product_variants.cost_price`) drift.                                                                    | Only `StockService` writes either, in the same transaction; `pos:stock-rebuild --costs` recomputes the blended figure; test asserts equality for single-location shops.                                                                                                                  |
| Backfill migrations on the live retail client (base units, `variant_prices.variant_unit_id`, `sale_items.location_id` / `base_quantity`, `stock_levels.average_cost`). | Chunked, idempotent migrations; rehearse on a copy of the retail DB; ship to retail only after branch 4 is stable.                                                                                                                                                                       |
| Partial pack returns: a box of 30 sold, 10 tablets brought back is 0.333 box.                                                                                          | Medicines are whole units only, so a part pack goes back as base units (10 tablets, never 0.333 box), stored in `sale_return_items.base_quantity`; the refund is proportional to base quantity, and the last return refunds the remainder (existing rule).                               |
| A wrong base unit chosen at setup can't be corrected after go-live (base unit locked).                                                                                 | Dosage-form defaults, a check of the unit columns in the import dry run, and a review of the item list with the pharmacist before opening stock is loaded.                                                                                                                               |
| Offline receipt shows a batch the server changed.                                                                                                                      | Honour valid proposals (§7); review list shows every change; reprint shows the recorded batch.                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| Batch and expiry data entry errors at GRN (wrong expiry = wrong FEFO).                                                                                                 | Expiry required and validated (not in the past, not > 10 years), confirmation for short expiry (shop setting; 90 days for the clinic), audited correction flow.                                                                                                                          |
| Catalog payload size with batches and two pools.                                                                                                                       | Only sellable batches with stock; delta sync already pages at 500.                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| Retail regressions from `StockService` refactor.                                                                                                                       | Non-batch path keeps `record()`'s signature and behaviour; existing `StockServiceTest`, `WeightedAverageCostTest`, `CheckoutServiceTest` stay unchanged and green in every branch.                                                                                                       |
| The cloud server or the clinic's internet fails (decided 2026-10-22: cloud, not a local server).                                                                       | Branch 12: nightly encrypted backups at another provider, verified weekly by a restore, restore drill, rollback; uptime monitor and health alerts; broadband with 4G failover and a UPS at the clinic; `offline_sync` (on, Q10) keeps the tills billing while the server is unreachable. |
| Oversold stock in the unallocated batch goes unnoticed.                                                                                                                | Alert on the dashboard and as a notification until settled; excluded from expiry reports so it never distorts them.                                                                                                                                                                      |
| Scope creep from Phase 2 (patients, dispensing labels) into checkout.                                                                                                  | Phase 1 keeps free-text Rx reference and `customer_name`; anything needing a patient record waits.                                                                                                                                                                                       |

## 12. Open questions

Q1–Q4, Q9, Q10, Q11 and Q16 were decided on 2026-10-18 (see [Decisions](#decisions-2026-10-18)); Q1, Q3, Q5, Q7, Q11, Q12, Q14 and Q15 were answered by the clinic on 2026-10-20 ([client answers](#client-answers-2026-10-20)), and Q6 and Q8 were decided the same day. Numbers are kept so references stay valid. Still open: only the register details under Q3.

1. ~~Who owns the Doctor's Medicines pool and the consultation fee?~~ **Decided:** one owner, the doctor (2026-10-20); per-location reports; no settlement report.
2. ~~One invoice per bill, or per pool?~~ **Decided:** one series per bill, receipt grouped by location.
3. ~~Controlled-drug register format?~~ **Decided:** generic register (§4.10), built but off by default, because the clinic has not confirmed it keeps one (2026-10-20). _Still open:_ whether they do, and if so its columns and layout.
4. ~~Pool access?~~ **Decided:** any location by default; per-location permission later.
5. ~~Returns policy for medicines?~~ **Answered (2026-10-20):** fridge items are decided per return; the return form's choice defaults to off for them (built).
6. ~~Consultation fee: fixed or typed per visit?~~ **Decided (2026-10-20):** pre-filled from the Consultation product's price, editable at the OPD till (§4.3, built in branch 8).
7. ~~Supplier payments?~~ **Answered (2026-10-20):** credit terms are used and unpaid invoices and due dates must be tracked: per-GRN paid status, due dates and statements, built in branch 6b ([as built](#as-built-branch-6b)), on top of the balance built in branch 6.
8. ~~Offline edge cases?~~ **Decided (2026-10-20):** accept and flag, never reject (disabled cashier, till clock far ahead or behind); both clocks kept; reports use server time beyond a tolerance (§7.5, built in branch 8).
9. ~~Cost precision?~~ **Decided:** DECIMAL(20,4) for unit costs; prices and totals stay integers.
10. ~~Is `offline_sync` needed?~~ **Decided:** on for the clinic.
11. ~~Tax?~~ **Decided:** per-product tax category `exempt` | `standard`, medicines default exempt (built in branch 3). **Answered (2026-10-20):** the clinic charges no VAT on sales (shop tax off, so the consultation fee's category doesn't matter); VAT on supplier invoices is part of the cost (built).
12. ~~"Do not sell within N days of expiry"?~~ **Answered (2026-10-20):** N = 0, sell until expiry; short-expiry warning at 90 days, editable.
13. ~~Pack prices?~~ **Decided:** every pack level and the loose unit have their own price; nothing is derived.
14. ~~Where is the bill rung up?~~ **Answered (2026-10-20):** two counters, one per pool, each till fixed to its location; users have allowed locations; no mixed-location bills. An OPD bill is the consultation plus one lump sum for the medicines (branch 8).
15. ~~Transfer between pools: sale or cost transfer?~~ **Answered (2026-10-20):** one owner, so a cost transfer (as built); all stock arrives at GEN and is transferred to DOC.
16. ~~Unit defaults and `allow_unit_sale`?~~ **Decided:** a product whose only unit is the bottle is always sellable; `allow_unit_sale` only governs larger packs (e.g. a carton); injections use vial / ampoule as the base unit and single vials are sellable.
